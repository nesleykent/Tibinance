# The user's brief (verbatim requirements, condensed only where marked)

Reconstruct the report around a single inferential chain and revise its language to meet the rigour, clarity and tone expected of a high standard McKinsey report. The revision must address both the structure of the argument and the quality of the prose. Short sentences and frequent paragraph breaks fragment the reasoning; the final report must use continuous prose, with well developed sentences, effective subordinating conjunctions and subordinate clauses that connect evidence, interpretation and conclusions naturally.

Produce both an English version and a Brazilian Portuguese version. In the Brazilian Portuguese version, all financial, statistical, Tibia and game specific terms must remain in English, applied consistently throughout the entire report rather than translating such terms selectively. Use LaTeX for formulas. Sources listed at the end, Chicago style for references and title capitalization.

NEVER use em dashes, and prevent any typographic glyph that resembles or mimics an em dash (added by the user mid-task).

Required single logical progression: research question -> data construction -> market characterisation -> temporal dynamics -> competing models -> out of sample validation -> prospective scenarios -> implications for execution -> heterogeneity across worlds -> robustness and limitations. Every section exists because it resolves a necessary stage of the inference and establishes the foundation required by the next.

1. Introduction: define the phenomenon, establish the research question, explain the contribution. Must NOT begin by stating how much Antica might be worth in November. Begin by defining the subject as the dynamics of Tibia Coin prices in gold, and explain that the analysis distinguishes trend, seasonality, cycles, heterogeneity across worlds and execution frictions. Must contain (anticipating the whole report) the statement: "We characterise the temporal and cross sectional dynamics of Tibia Coin prices in gold, evaluate the predictive capacity of competing models through out of sample validation and, conditional on that evidence, construct prospective scenarios and examine their implications under real execution frictions."
2. Data and variable construction: what is measured and how variables are constructed: monitored worlds, modelled worlds, captures (the user quoted 24/23/98 from an older edition; the page reads counts from data, currently 28/28/102), observation period, Sell Offers, Buy Offers, day_average, cleaning criteria, mergers, predecessor worlds, Floribra, Luzibra, definition of inflation in Tibia Coins. Every decision that determines what is measured must precede the results.
3. Empirical structure of the market: begin with observed patterns in price levels, spreads, market depth and differences across worlds, then Tibia Coin inflation, temporal trends and seasonality; motivate modelling choices.
4. Temporal dynamics and cycles: turning points, 5 per cent reversals, cycle duration, cycle magnitude (is the recent movement consistent with history or a departure); volatility, autocorrelation, forward returns in the same section; a unified interpretation, not isolated analyses.
5. Modelling strategy: constant, seasonal, harmonic and ARIMA with harmonic components as competing specifications, each with a clear empirical rationale following from the preceding sections; not a catalogue.
6. Validation before projections: MAPE, fortnightly backtests, interval coverage, Brier score, sensitivity to estimation window, sensitivity to seed; establish how much predictive credibility each model has.
7. Prospective scenarios: November 2026, June 2027, central scenario, stress bands, P10 to P90 distribution, timing of peaks; the distinction between forecast, scenario and simulation explicit throughout.
8. Economic and execution implications: selling, repurchasing, maker and taker execution, fees, round trip cost, probabilities of gains in Tibia Coins, as applications of the estimated process, not inputs into estimating it. Answer: given the estimated process, what do market frictions do to an executable strategy?
9. Heterogeneity across worlds: relative premiums, correlations, relative volatility, level shifts, merger effects, with Antica as benchmark; a cross sectional extension that does not interrupt the central temporal argument.
10. Robustness and complementary analyses: day of week effects, events, placebos, calendar effects, daily averages, alternative specifications; prominence proportional to whether they materially affect the core findings.
11. Discussion: integrate evidence without repeating tables or reopening analyses; what results support regarding TC inflation, persistence, seasonality, predictability, heterogeneity, execution costs; carefully separate observational evidence from economic interpretation.
12. Conclusion: answer the research question directly; no new results, no reopened analyses, scenarios not deterministic predictions; close the inferential chain.
